    1. What Are Dark Patterns?

    Dark patterns are user interface (UI) and user experience (UX) designs crafted meticulously to trick users into doing things they might not otherwise do, such as signing up for recurring billing, sharing excessive personal data, or agreeing to hidden privacy waivers. Coined originally by UX expert Harry Brignull, these patterns exploit cognitive biases.

    2. Common Types of Deceptive Design Traps

    • Roach Motel: Making it extremely easy to get into a situation (like signing up or subscribing) but making the cancellation process an absolute maze of hidden buttons and customer support hoops.
    • Sneak into Basket: Adding extra items or insurance to a user's cart without explicit affirmative consent, relying on the user not noticing during checkout.
    • Forced Continuity: Free trials that automatically convert into expensive monthly subscriptions without sending a clear notification reminder before charging the credit card.
    • Confirmshaming: Using guilt-tripping language on pop-up opt-out buttons (e.g., "No thanks, I hate saving money") to manipulate user choices.
